# Qwen3.8-27B on QEngine 1.5.0 — the club 8-pack, two KV precisions, and a batched-path fix

*2026-11-26 · a bench report, not a product. Companion to `BENCH.md`; every cell below names its build, its pack
versions and its draw count.*

---

## 0. The two questions this answers

1. **Does the engine's quality change when one library file is replaced by our build** — the same image, the same
   pack, the same seats and window, one `libqengine.so` different — where the difference is a fix to an attention
   copy in the **batched** step?
2. **Is a borrowed KV calibration still right at depth?** 32 BF16 `k_scale`/`v_scale` scalars lifted from QEngine's
   INT4 checkpoint, lent to a mixed NVFP4/FP8 pack that ships none of its own, in exchange for **twice the KV pool**.

**Answers, in one line each.**

- **No measurable quality change on either thinking leg**, and that is now a *placed* statement rather than an
  impression: the cross-arm deltas are smaller than the same arm's own draw-to-draw churn (measured below).
- **The pack suite cannot see the fix at all** — not even on the pack where the fault fires — because it issues one
  request at a time and never enters `step_batch`. The fix's effect is measured with a different instrument, where it
  is unambiguous: **stock returns corrupted text on the batched path, ours returns the right text.**
- **The borrowed calibration doubles the pool with no recall loss to 89.7 K** on a fixed-seed needle — a bound, not
  a fidelity claim.

## 2. The headline — stock vs ours, both legs

`RedHatAI/Qwen3.8-27B-INT4` (the pack the shipped QEngine composes use), thinking off and thinking on:

| leg | stock | ours | same-arm floor | cross-arm |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| **OFF** (instruct) | **112** then **110** | **116** | **Δ2 total, 20 scenario flips** | **+4 on 16 flips** |
| **ON** (thinking, effort `low`) | **132** | **129**, **129** | **Δ0 total, 12 flips** | **−3 on 9 flips** |

**Read it the way the instrument demands** — at scenario level, against the floor:

- the OFF delta is **+4 items riding on 16 flips**, while two draws of the *same* arm differ by **20 flips** and
  swap 2 items. The delta is smaller than the instrument's own churn → **indistinguishable**.
- the ON delta is **−3 on 9 flips**, and our arm reproduced its total exactly (129, 129) across two draws with 12
  flips between them. → **indistinguishable**, placed rather than asserted.
- the two legs disagree in direction (+4 and −3), which is what noise looks like.

## 3. Why the pack suite cannot see the fix

The engine schedules `step()` for a lone decoder and `step_batch()` for two or more. benchlocal issues one request at
a time, so **no scenario ever enters the batched entry** — the fix's code path is never executed, on any pack.

That is testable, not just arguable. Run the same two builds on `primitive-ai/Qwen3.8-27B-mixed-NVFP4-FP8` — the one
pack here whose KV carries **no scales**, i.e. the one where the fault is live — with loop guards off:

| build | total | flips |
|---|---:|---:|
| pre-fix | **111/150** | 13 (6 lost, 7 gained) vs ours |
| ours | **112/150** | — |

**A wash, on the pack where the defect fires.** So the pack run's role is exactly one thing: *the fix costs no
quality*. It is not, and cannot be, evidence about the fix.

## 4. The mechanism, measured both ways

Two instruments that *do* take the batched path, on the same pack, same image, same trace server, one library file:

| instrument | pre-fix build | our build |
|---|---|---|
| one request forced through `step_batch` | **FAIL** — `OCH-H1A2E49-2019.1`, 16 tok | **PASS** — `OCHRE-3391`, 9 tok |
| two concurrent requests (production path, no trace switch) | slot 0 correct 2/2, **slot 1 DEGRADED 2/2** (`VIO-7788` for `VIOLET-7788`) | **4/4 correct** |

The failing reply is the planted string with an adapter token fused into it (`OCH` + `H1A2E49-2019.1`) — a stale copy
surfacing, not a refusal and not a truncation. That is the defect, reproduced in both directions on one machine.

**Mechanism, from source.** The attention segment writes its fp16 copy of the attention output only on its
`fp8-KV` branch; a checkpoint with no `k_scale`/`v_scale` takes a fallback path that never writes it, while the
batched caller hands that buffer to the output GEMV regardless. The fix makes the fp8 condition explicit at both
batched call sites. **Consequence, and it is the reason the two NVFP4/INT8 packs look clean on stock:** a pack that
*does* ship KV scales runs the fp8 branch, where the copy is written — so the fault needs a pack without them.

## 5. Three packs, one band

All rows thinking off, guards off, one draw, `--strict-thinking`:

| pack | engine | KV | total | notes |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| `RedHatAI/Qwen3.8-27B-INT4` | stock | fp8 | **112 / 110** | the shipped composes' weights; pool 1,022,976 tok |
| `bpx03/Qwen3.8-27B-INT8` | stock | fp8 | **111** | pool 672,768 tok; loads in 171 s against INT4's 18 s warm |
| `primitive-ai/…-mixed-NVFP4-FP8` | ours (gate + build) | bf16 | **112** | only our gate opens it; pool 553,984 tok |

**One band, no separation.** The INT8 pack's own model card says the 8-pack "has never run on these weights" and
asks whether its much lower KL (6.66e-4 against INT4's 1.47e-2, ≈22× closer to BF16) buys better answers in real
tasks. On this instrument it does not: **111 against 110–112**, with the two cross-pack pairs differing by one item on
23 and 31 flips respectively. Cross-pack rows are *shape records* — format plus recipe differ — so this is a finding
about the instrument's resolution as much as about the formats.

## 6. The KV leg — a borrowed calibration at depth

Two authors on one pack: the weights are `primitive-ai`'s, the 32 BF16 scales are QEngine's INT4 checkpoint
(`kvscales`, 3,360 B file, 64 B of data). Same build, same prompts, **KV precision the only variable**:

| arm | the engine's own line | KV pool | 10,038 | 29,928 | 59,828 | 89,663 |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| bf16 (the pack's own state) | `no FP8 KV scales in the checkpoint: bf16 KV cache` | 553,984 | pass | pass | pass | pass |
| borrowed INT4 calibration | `KV cache scales from kvscales.safetensors` | **1,107,968** | pass | pass | pass | pass |

The needle is a fixed-seed construction (byte-identical payload for a given seed/blocks, and it prints the payload
hash), so the two arms were asked the same question at the same place: the hashes match at every depth
(`0d767e43 · 29aabc24 · 59c1facc · 7d745168`). **The 2× pool costs nothing on this instrument to 89.7 K.**

**What it does not say.** Needle recall tests retrieval against one planted fact: it is coarse, one draw per cell, and
blind to a distributional shift. The dense instrument (KL against a named reference) is not built here: the HTTP
surface caps `top_logprobs` at 20 entries per token, and a KL truncated to the top 20 cannot resolve the ~2e-4 floor
this kind of question needs. So this leg is a **bound** — it rules out a gross long-context failure from a borrowed
calibration; it does not certify it.

## 7. Rate — free on the path the fix touches, and a price that cannot be measured honestly

`bench.sh` (narrative + code, 3 warm-up + 5 measured each, decode mean), **A B A B** with a fresh boot per run,
because the harness's own header warns that a single sequential pair cannot separate the patch from the position:

| run | build | decode mean | CV | TTFT |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | pre-fix | 346.24 tok/s | 2.3 % | 22 ms |
| B1 | ours | 346.51 | 1.9 % | 22 ms |
| A2 | pre-fix | 346.22 | 3.3 % | 22 ms |
| B2 | ours | 344.33 | 3.2 % | 22 ms |

Arms: **A 346.23 · B 345.42 → −0.23 %**, which is smaller than the B arm's own draw-to-draw spread (0.63 %). On the
single-stream path the fix is free — expected, since that path is `step()` and the fix touches only `step_batch`.

**And the batched price is not measurable against the broken arm.** Under `step_batch` the pre-fix build returns
wrong text, so its rate is the rate of a broken workload — a workload the fault re-shapes cannot price a fix to that
fault. A correct comparison needs two *correct* arms (fix on, versus a build that repairs the read but skips the
copy); that build does not exist. So the cost is stated as a **bound**: provably zero where the fp8 branch already
writes the copy, and one copy of the attention output per attention layer on the bf16 path, by construction.

## 8. What the stock gate accepts (and what ours adds)

Booting the mixed NVFP4/FP8 pack on the stock image, unmodified, exits at the start gate with its own explanation:

> *(model path elided)* `sharded checkpoint, no model.safetensors. The 27B engine reads one model.safetensors in the compressed-tensors`
> `pack-quantized layout: INT4 or symmetric INT8, group 128, weight_packed int32 [N][K/8] or [N][K/4] with the stored`
> `value w + offset, weight_scale bf16 [N][K/128]. tools/quant_int8.py builds an INT8 one from the BF16 weights.`

So the stock accepted set is **one file, INT4 or symmetric INT8, group 128**. The delta between that sentence and
"this pack loads and scores 112/150" is exactly two things: **sharded sets** (read `index.json`, merge every shard's
header into one name map) and the **NVFP4 / FP8 foreign layouts**. Not a faster engine — a set of checkpoints the
stock engine cannot open at all.

## 9. Caveats, stated so nobody has to guess

- **One draw per leg**, except where a table shows two. The single-draw rows carry a within-arm floor from the same
  arm at the same settings; the ON floor (12 flips, Δ0) is from our arm only — the stock arm's second ON draw was
  lost to a harness race and is owed.
- **Every comparator is a pair, not a row**, unless the floor is on the same page. Where the loop guard was left on
  (an early pair, not reported here), both legs read `validity=CONTAMINATED` and neither total is quotable.
- **`validity=valid` covers seven of the eight packs.** The harness's per-pack thinking check has no entry for the
  sandboxed agent pack, so reasoning inside it is never counted — every leg here carries a few hundred to two
  thousand reasoning tokens there, on guards-off legs too, i.e. it is the pack's own, not the guard's.
- **Effort is fixed** at the engine's `low` on thinking legs; a different effort is a different cell.
- **Nothing here is a cross-format verdict.** Every cross-pack row above is a shape record: format *and* recipe
  differ between packs, so only same-pack comparisons carry a verdict.
- **The drafter is on** (DFlash2). It is lossless for quality and irrelevant to the pack cells; it does affect rate.
- **Not measured:** `--repeat 3` on any cell (the published rows' draw count), the vendor pack's rate cells, the
  loader's dtype/scale-block acceptance checks, and any KL number.

## 10. Reproducing it

The harness is the club's, unmodified — one leg is one command, pointed at whatever is serving:

```bash
# one leg: thinking off, strict, all eight packs, one draw
URL=http://<host>:<port> MODEL=<served-id> \
  bash scripts/quality-test.sh --full --no-thinking --strict-thinking --save-json leg-off.json

# the same leg with thinking on (effort low is set at boot on this engine)
URL=http://<host>:<port> MODEL=<served-id> \
  bash scripts/quality-test.sh --full --enable-thinking --strict-thinking --save-json leg-on.json
```

Boot settings that matter for comparability, all read back from the container rather than the caller's line: **one
boot per leg**, loop guards off (`QE_LOOP_GUARD_TURNS=0`, `QE_LOOP_GUARD_THINK=0`, `QE_LOOP_GUARD_NOTE=0`), effort
left unset for an OFF leg and `low` for an ON leg, 8 seats, and — for the three sandboxed packs — an endpoint
published where a container can reach it.

Read the results at **scenario level** (`packs[].scenarios[].id` and `.pass_at_k`), never on the total alone: two
draws of the same arm here moved 20 verdicts of 150 while swapping 2 items.
